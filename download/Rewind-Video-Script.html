Video Production Script
REWIND
THE APP
A 2-Minute Promotional Video Script
DURATION: 2:00  ·  FORMAT: 1080p  ·  STYLE: Nostalgic Cinematic
Total Scenes: 10  ·  Music: Lo-fi / Synthwave Blend  ·  Voice: Warm, Conversational
Target Audience: Millennials & Gen Z  ·  Tone: Emotional, Playful, Wistful
Overview
The Story We Are Telling
This script follows a single emotional arc: the viewer enters their birth year and is transported back to the sights, sounds, and culture of their formative years. The video opens with a mysterious, dark interface that sparks to life. As the decade theme activates, the entire visual language shifts. We see curated movies, a vintage camera, retro games, an AIM-style chat, a personal timeline, and a memory gallery. The closing lands on an emotional note about the power of nostalgia and the importance of holding onto who you are.
The pacing mirrors a VHS tape being inserted and played. Each scene transition uses CRT flickers, scanlines, or tape-tracking artifacts. The music starts sparse and ambient, builds through the decade reveal, peaks during the feature montage, and resolves to a soft, warm close. The voiceover is warm and conversational, like a friend showing you something they love, never corporate or salesy.

SCENE 01 0:00 - 0:10
Cold Open - The Dark Screen
VISUAL: Black screen. Slowly, green CRT text appears character by character, like a computer booting: "BIOS v2.4 -- Nostalgia Systems Inc." then "Detecting memories... 640K OK." A faint synthwave grid fades in from the bottom. The Rewind logo materializes pixel by pixel in the center. A soft glow pulses around it.
VO: "What if you could go back? Not just remember, but feel it again. The sounds. The colors. The person you were.">
SFX: Low CRT hum, faint dial-up static. Music: Single sustained synth pad, barely audible. Camera: Static wide shot, slow zoom in.
SCENE 02 0:10 - 0:18
The Question
VISUAL: The boot text fades out. A clean, elegant input field appears: "What year were you born?" A cursor blinks. A hand types "1990" (or whichever year). The screen pauses for a beat. Then a VHS tracking glitch tears across the screen. The loading screen kicks in: cassette reels spin, progress bar fills chunkily. Text reads: "Rewinding tape..." The decade title slams in: "THE 90s" with a Windows 95-style title bar.
VO: "Enter your birth year. And just like that, the world shifts. Your world. The one you grew up in."
SFX: Keyboard clacks, VHS tracking glitch, cassette rewind. Music: Tempo picks up slightly, adds a lo-fi drum pattern. Camera: Tight on the input, then pull back for the theme reveal.
SCENE 03 0:18 - 0:30
The Dashboard Reveal
VISUAL: The full dashboard appears with a smooth fade. We see the wood-grain header, the cork-board background, the decade badge. The camera slowly pans across the tab bar: Reel, Movies, Journal, Games, Contacts, Memory, Timeline. Each tab lights up briefly as the camera passes. The hero section shows the decade name and tagline. The whole interface has transformed -- if it was the 80s, everything is neon and dark. If 90s, gray beveled windows. If 2000s, glossy blue gradients.
VO: "This is Rewind. A personal time machine that rebuilds your entire childhood in one screen. Every movie. Every game. Every memory. Curated just for you, from your first year to eighteen."
SFX: UI click sounds, subtle whoosh on tab highlights. Music: Full lo-fi beat drops here. Camera: Slow dolly across the interface, shallow depth of field on foreground elements.

VO: "Browse 111 curated movies from your era, with real posters and ratings. Write in your journal with a vintage fountain pen. Play the games you grew up with -- Minesweeper, Tic-Tac-Toe, Word Search. All styled to match your decade."
SFX: Page turn for journal, game sound effects. Music: Beat continues, add playful melody line. Camera: Quick cuts (1-2 sec each), smooth transitions. Each feature demo is 5-7 seconds.

VO: "Dial a rotary phone and hear it ring. Chat on AIM with a dial-up connection. Take retro photos with real vintage filters. Every moment gets saved in your Memory gallery -- photos from the camera or from your own device."
SFX: Phone dial clicks, ring, dial-up modem sounds, camera shutter. Music: Peak energy here, most dynamic section. Camera: Mix of close-ups on interactions and wider contextual shots.

Production Notes
Technical and Creative Guidelines
Music Direction
Use a lo-fi / synthwave blend that shifts by decade. 80s sections lean into analog synths and arpeggiated basslines. 90s sections add grunge-influenced guitar textures and drum loops. 2000s sections brighten with clean electric piano and polished production. The emotional close should strip back to a single piano or ambient pad. License from Artlist, Epidemic Sound, or similar. Total music cost target: under $50.
Voice-Over Casting
Cast a warm, conversational voice -- think documentary narrator, not commercial announcer. Age 28-40 preferred. The tone should feel like a friend showing you something personal, never salesy or corporate. Pace should be unhurried, with natural pauses. American English, neutral accent. Record at 44.1kHz, 16-bit WAV. Budget approximately $100-200 on platforms like Voices.com or Fiverr.
Recording and Editing
Record the app screen at 1080p using a screen capture tool (OBS, ScreenFlow, or system built-in). For the three-decade split section, record the same dashboard three times with different birth years. Add CRT/glitch transitions in post using overlays (scanline PNG, tracking distortion, VHS color shift). Use subtle camera movements in post (digital zoom, slow pan, slight parallax on UI layers). Target a 1-2 second hold on each feature showcase for readability. Export at H.264, 30fps, 8-12 Mbps for web delivery.
Distribution
Primary: Instagram Reels / TikTok (9:16 vertical crop, 60-second highlights). Secondary: YouTube (16:9 full version, 2:00). Tertiary: App Store / Play Store preview (30-second supercut of best moments). Create a 15-second teaser for Twitter/X and LinkedIn. All versions should feel native to their platform while maintaining the retro aesthetic.
